Out of 10 crochet projects I've made from the cheapest worsted-weight yarn the craft store sells, the one that paid me best for my time came out of a bag of leftover scraps I'd have been ashamed to show anybody. But the one I was proudest of, a lacy afghan that ate up most of two weeks in my rocking chair, finished dead last at about a dollar-fifty an hour.

I'm Granny Mae, and this one's for anybody sitting at a kitchen table with a hook, a skein of yarn, and a stack of stitch books, wondering if all that handwork is really worth anything on a bazaar table.

Start with the one that lost. A shell-stitch afghan is one of those big lacy throws with the fans of stitches marching across it in rows, the kind you see folded over the back of the good couch. Mine was about four feet by five, a plain shell pattern with a simple border. It ate eight skeins at about $3 apiece, and everywhere the pattern shells out, you have to count your stitches dead on or the whole thing leans off to one side. I sat with that afghan through two weeks of evening television, and when I added it all up, it paid me about a dollar-fifty an hour.

Now the winner. Scrap-yarn coasters, made from the ends in that bag under my table. By the time the scarves and the dishcloths were all done, that bag was stuffed full of yarn ends in every color under the sun, and it was sitting right next to the trash can. I was about to carry the whole thing out when I thought, well now, why not work a few of these ends into something?

Work a snug little round in single crochet till it's about four inches across, tuck in the two ends, and give it a firm press, and that's the whole thing. It takes me about ten minutes apiece, and the cost is nothing at all, because it's yarn I'd already paid for. Every one comes out a little different, and folks liked digging through the basket to pick out their favorites.

They sell for $4 to $6 apiece, or four for $15 as a set, and I sold every last one in a single afternoon. A scrap of free yarn and ten minutes coming back around five dollars comes out to about $30 an hour. That's twenty times what the lace afghan paid me, out of a bag of ends I'd nearly thrown out.

My grandmother used to say a plain thing done neat will always outsell a fancy thing done slow, and a bag of scrap coasters proved her right all over again. Keep that hook moving, and mind you don't lose your place. I'll see you back here at the kitchen table.
