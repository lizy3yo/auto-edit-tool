Here's something that makes no sense until it does. The woman who spends the most money and the most time on her hair is usually the one who reads oldest in the room.

Not because she is older. Because she is trying harder, and it shows.

And sitting across the table from her is a French woman of exactly the same age who washed her hair the day before yesterday, ran her fingers through it on the way out, and looks like she is getting away with something.

I'm Diane, and this channel is for women over sixty who would like to stop fighting. Three habits today, and every one of them is something women do deliberately, believing it helps. The last one is the cheapest, and it changes the most. I am saving it.

Number one. The one-tone dark dye. A woman notices the gray arriving at her part, and she reaches for the shade she wore at forty. Root to tip, one flat color, refreshed every four weeks, forever. Real hair carries eight or ten tones at once, which is why it moves in the light. A dye job carries one. The hair looks applied.

French women stay within a shade or two of whatever is actually growing out of their head. And when the gray comes, it is allowed in. Blended through, then glossed so it catches light.

Number two. The daily blow dry. Wash, towel, dryer, brush, every single morning. Hair that gets this treatment goes thin through the ends, and it develops a fine halo of frizz around the crown.

Washing less is the fix. Your scalp produces oil on a schedule it learned from you. Wash it twice a week and it recalibrates inside a month. For the days in between, dry shampoo. Not on the hair. On the scalp. Then wait two full minutes before you touch anything.

Number three, the cheapest one. Skipping the gloss.

A gloss is not a dye. No lift, no bleach, no roots to manage afterward. It is a clear or lightly tinted treatment that seals the cuticle down flat, and makes light bounce off it in one direction instead of scattering in forty. Silver goes brassy and yellow over time, and a gloss with a cool tone under it neutralizes that in about twenty minutes. At home, a gloss kit is around $12.

Shine is the single thing people read as healthy hair. A woman with gray hair that catches the light will read younger than a woman with flawlessly dyed brown hair that goes flat under a restaurant lamp.

Now look at what those three have in common. Less dye. Less heat. The only thing I asked you to add was a gloss. Every single fix was a subtraction.
