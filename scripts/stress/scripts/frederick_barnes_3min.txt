Fires don't all behave the same way. One is the fast one. Grease catches on the stove, and there's open flame in seconds. If you're standing there awake, you don't need a machine to tell you about it. The other one is the slow one. A cord under a rug. A space heater quietly cooking the side of a mattress at 2 in the morning. It smolders, and it puts out thick, greasy smoke for a long stretch before there's a single flame in the room, while everyone in the house is asleep. And once today's foam furniture does catch, a bedroom goes from smoking to fully involved in about 3 minutes.

So the alarm's real job was never the flame. Its job is catching the slow part.

First thing on the label. The type. Ionization is quick on open flame, and it's what's in most of the cheap multipacks. Photoelectric sees the slow, smoldering fire substantially earlier. Earlier in a way that decides whether anybody wakes up on their own. So get a stepladder, take one alarm down, and read the label. If it carries a notice about radioactive material, that's ionization, and I'd plan on replacing it. Photoelectric, or dual sensor, is what you want.
