Put two ribbed hats side by side, one knitted on a pair of needles and one made on a ring of 41 plastic pegs, and on a cold morning your ears couldn't tell you which one they're under. They're made of the very same stitches, the knit and the purl. That's the hat you're making today, and you won't touch a knitting needle once.

Hello, friend, and welcome to Pearl Parker. I'm Pearl, and this lesson is for you if the needles already beat you once, if your fingers have gotten stiff and don't care for fiddly work anymore. Five steps will take you from an empty loom to a warm ribbed hat.

Step one is your loom and your yarn. On a round loom, the number of pegs decides the size of your hat. The biggest loom in the inexpensive sets, with 40 or 41 pegs, is the one for a grown-up's hat. The pegs sit a good way apart, so they want thick yarn, a 5 or a 6 on the label. If your yarn is worsted weight, a 4, hold it double, knitting from both ends of the same skein.

Step two is the cast on. Tie your yarn to the anchor peg, the small one sticking out from the side. Then give every peg one full circle of yarn, clockwise, that crosses over itself in a small X. Snug, but loose enough to slide your hook underneath.

Then your first knitting. Wrap the first peg again, so it's wearing two loops. Slide your hook under the bottom loop, lift it up, over the top loop, and off the peg. I call that the leapfrog. Wrap, then leapfrog, and you've made a knit stitch.

Step three is the brim. Knit one peg and purl the next, over and over. That's ribbing. The purl is the scoop. Bring the yarn across the inside of the peg, under its loop. Push your hook down through that loop, catch the yarn, and draw a small loop up. Lift the old loop off, and slip the new one onto the peg. A purl peg has a little collar of yarn at its base, so the pegs tell you which is which. Keep going until the brim is about 2 inches deep.

Step four is the body. Every peg gets a knit now, round after round. For a grown-up, knit until the tube measures 8 inches from the bottom of the brim. And measure before you close the top. The first loom hat I ever closed, I closed on a guess, and it sat on my head like a thimble on a thumb.

Step five is the top. Cut the yarn with a long tail, and before a single loop comes off, run that tail through the loop on every peg. Then lift the loops off and pull. The top gathers in like the neck of a drawstring bag. Knot it on the inside, weave the ends away, and turn it right side out.

Every stitch in that hat is a knit or a purl, and on a cold morning, your ears won't have the faintest idea it came off a loom.
