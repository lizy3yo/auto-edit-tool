Look at the floor beside your toilet. That little stretch of tile between the bowl and the wall. In most American bathrooms, it is empty. In a German bathroom, usually, a small bin stands there, with a lid. It is the answer to one plain sentence the water utilities in Germany have repeated for years: the toilet is not a trash can.

Nine things stay out of the toilet there, and in plenty of American homes, most of them go down it in an ordinary week. One has a word printed on its package that your pipes do not agree with. One is a thread so thin you would never think it could stop anything, and it can stop a pump a long way from your house.

And besides what your own body sends there, exactly one thing is allowed in the bowl. It was built on purpose to do something the nine cannot. I am Werner Finkbeiner. This channel is for people in older houses and apartments who would rather understand the cause than pay for the visit. Today we go through all nine, one at a time, and look at what the German habit does instead.

So. We will go the way the water goes. Flush, and follow. Each of the nine stops somewhere, and each one stops a little farther from your house than the one before.

Number one never leaves the toilet. Cotton swabs. The little stick with cotton on both ends. Here is the part nobody sees. Inside the porcelain, the water does not fall straight down. It climbs up and over a curve molded into the toilet itself. That curve is called the trapway, and it is what holds water in the bowl and keeps the sewer smell out of your bathroom.

Good design. But a stiff stick does not always follow a curve. It can turn sideways and catch across the passage, like a bar across a doorway. One swab, maybe nothing happens. But now there is a bar in the doorway. The next piece of paper catches on it. Then the next. The toilet starts flushing slow, and you decide the toilet is getting old.

The toilet is fine. There is a stick in it. If you think one is already in there, the plunger is the wrong tool. It can push the stick deeper into the curve. A toilet auger, the short kind made for toilets, can sometimes hook it out. If it will not come, the toilet has to come up off the floor, and that is a fair place to stop and call someone.
