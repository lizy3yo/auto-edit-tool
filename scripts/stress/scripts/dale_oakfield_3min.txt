You finished a walnut and maple cutting board a few weeks back. It's still leaning against the wall in your garage. That happens to everybody who sells what they build, and it's fixable, because the board usually isn't the problem. The place you tried to sell it just wasn't built for that piece. Today I'm ranking Etsy, craft fairs, and Facebook Marketplace by what each one actually does well. And hang on for the Etsy fee that can more than double what comes out of a single sale.

I'm Dale Oakfield, and this is my workshop. This one's for the woodworker with finished pieces piling up and no clear idea where they'll sell.

Number one. Sort your finished pieces by size and price before you pick a place to sell them. Three piles. Smalls, about $10 to $40. Coasters, ornaments, small cutting boards. Personalized mid-tier work, $50 to $200. Engraved boards, house signs, keepsake boxes. And furniture, $150 to $600. Once every piece has a pile, half of the selling decision is already made for you.

Number two. Use Etsy for the thing it does best, which is reach. Your shop is open at two in the morning, and a buyer three states away, hunting for a board with her parents' last name on it, can find yours while you're sound asleep. That's why the personalized pile belongs on Etsy first.

Number three, the one I promised you. Etsy's regular fees take roughly $10 off a $100 board. But if a shopper clicks one of Etsy's ads on another site and buys from you within 30 days, that order carries an extra fee of 15%. Now somewhere around $25 is gone on one board. Below $10,000 a year in sales, you can opt out in your shop settings. Either way, leave enough room in the price that an ad sale still pays you.

Number four. Take your smalls to craft fairs and farmers markets. Coasters and ornaments are impulse buys. People decide on them because they're already holding one. Put the $10 to $40 items along the front edge of the table where hands reach first. One good Saturday market can gross $500 to $1,500.

Number five. Sell your furniture on Facebook Marketplace. Shipping a bookcase eats the profit, and hauling one to a fair eats the booth. Marketplace charges nothing to list for local pickup, and the buyer comes to you with a truck. Put the measurements in the very first line, and move the piece out to your driveway before pickup so strangers aren't walking through your house.

So don't pick one. Run all three, and let each one carry its own lane. Smalls to the markets, personalized work on Etsy, furniture on Marketplace. Every channel has a weak spot, and the other two cover for it.
