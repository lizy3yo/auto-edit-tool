Number one. The super-sniffer.

Before we talk about what your dog is reading, we have to talk about what he's reading it with. Because listen to this. You and I have somewhere around six million scent receptors up in our noses. Your dog has roughly three hundred million.

Three hundred million. Against our six.

So when your dog plants his snout against your leg, he isn't getting a little whiff. He's reading the whole newspaper, front page to the comics.

Number two. The travel log.

Everywhere you go, little bits of the world hitch a ride home with you. The parking lot rubs off on your shoes. The grocery store clings to your coat. You can't smell any of that. He can smell every bit of it.

So here's a simple way to work with that nose. You can practice this tonight.

Make your arrival boring. Come in quietly. Set your keys down. Keep your voice low and easy.

Then stand still and let the sniff happen. Let your hands hang low and relaxed. Let him check your shoes, your legs, your fingers. For a lot of dogs, that's somewhere around ten or fifteen seconds, and once the read is finished, you'll see the body loosen up.

Then say hello. Soft voice, calm pat on the chest or the side of the neck. Just, "Hey, buddy. I'm home."

That long, careful sniff is his way of saying, "Tell me about your day." He just doesn't need you to use words.
